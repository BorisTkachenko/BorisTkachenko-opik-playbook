# Hollowbrook Garden Care Guide

A fictional guide for the cool, damp valley gardens around Hollowbrook (hardiness zone 6b, last frost around 12 May, first frost around 20 October).

## Planting calendar: cool season

Sow peas, spinach, radishes and broad beans outdoors from 20 March, as soon as the soil crumbles instead of clumping. Start onion sets and potatoes on 5 April. Lettuce can be sown every two weeks until 1 June, then again from 15 August for an autumn crop. Cover seedlings with fleece if the forecast drops below 2 C.

## Planting calendar: warm season

Tomatoes, courgettes, runner beans and sweetcorn must wait until after the last frost on 12 May. Start tomatoes and courgettes indoors on 1 April in 9 cm pots, then harden them off for seven days on a sheltered step. Plant out in the evening and water in well. Basil sulks below 10 C, so it goes out last, around 25 May.

## Soil: clay and drainage

Hollowbrook valley soil is heavy clay. Never dig it wet: a handful that forms a ribbon is too wet to work. Fork in 5 cm of composted bark and a scoop of horticultural grit per square metre each autumn. Raised beds 25 cm deep fix most drainage problems. Avoid sand, which turns clay into something like concrete.

## Soil: feeding and pH

Test pH every second spring. Most vegetables want 6.0 to 7.0. Add garden lime in autumn if the reading is under 6.0, at about 100 g per square metre. Mulch with 5 cm of leaf mould in November. Hollowbrook Gold liquid feed (high potash) goes on tomatoes every ten days once the first truss has set fruit.

## Watering

Water deeply twice a week instead of lightly every day. A bed needs about 20 litres per square metre per week in July. Water early in the morning at the base of the plant, never over the leaves in the evening, because damp foliage invites blight. Pots dry faster and may need water daily in a heatwave.

## Pests: slugs and snails

Slugs love the valley's damp springs. Beer traps sunk to the rim, copper tape on raised beds and evening hand-picking work better than anything else. Water in nematode slug control from April to October when soil is above 5 C. Seedlings of lettuce, hostas and courgettes are the first to vanish.

## Pests: aphids and cabbage white

Aphids cluster on new growth from May. Blast them off with a jet of water, then encourage ladybirds by leaving a patch of nettles. Cabbage white butterflies lay yellow eggs under brassica leaves from June, so cover brassicas with 1 mm mesh and check weekly for eggs. Crush any you find.

## Diseases: blight and mildew

Tomato and potato blight arrives in humid weather in late July. Remove affected leaves at once, never compost them, and pick tomatoes early if brown patches spread. Powdery mildew on courgettes and squash is a drought stress symptom: water deeply and remove the worst leaves. Spacing plants 45 cm apart improves airflow.

## Autumn jobs

Lift potatoes by 20 September, before slugs find them. Sow green manure (field beans or winter rye) on empty beds in October. Plant garlic and spring bulbs from 15 October. Clean pots and tools with hot water before storing them. Leave seed heads standing for birds, and cut perennials back in March instead.
