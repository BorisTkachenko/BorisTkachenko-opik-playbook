# Gallery 2 — The Foghorn Gallery

**Placard 2.1 — Two notes, a minute apart**

The Skerrow Point foghorn sounded two notes every sixty seconds: a low blast of three seconds followed by a higher blast of one second. Ships learned this pattern as the signature of Skerrow Point, so a captain who heard it in thick fog knew exactly which headland was close.

**Placard 2.2 — The compressed-air engine**

The horn was driven by two Hornsby oil engines installed in 1911, each feeding a tank of compressed air. In heavy fog the engines ran for up to 40 hours without a break. Keepers kept a written record of every fog hour, because the coastal board paid the station by the hour of fog worked.

**Placard 2.3 — Press the brass button**

The foghorn in this gallery has been silenced for good. The brass button on the rail plays a recording made on 14 March 1974, the last day the real horn was sounded before the station was automated. The recording lasts 48 seconds.
