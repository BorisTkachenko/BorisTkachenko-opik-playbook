# Gallery 3 — The Keepers' Logbook

**Placard 3.1 — A watch is four hours**

Three keepers lived at Skerrow Point and shared the night in watches of four hours each. The principal keeper took the watch from midnight to four, because it was judged the loneliest. The lamp had to be trimmed every two hours, and a keeper who fell asleep on watch could be dismissed.

**Placard 3.2 — The entry of 9 November 1921**

The open page in the case is the logbook entry for 9 November 1921. It reads: "Gale from the north-west, force 11. Lamp kept burning. Heard cries at 2 a.m. but could see nothing." The principal keeper that night was Ismay Tolland, one of only four women known to have held the post in the whole country.

**Placard 3.3 — Pay and provisions**

A principal keeper earned 94 pounds a year in 1921, with a cottage, a coal allowance and a ration of tinned meat delivered every six weeks by supply boat. Assistant keepers earned 61 pounds. Families were allowed to live at the station, but the school on the mainland was a three-hour boat trip away.
