# Gallery 1 — The Great Lens

**Placard 1.1 — The Pellwick First-Order Lens**

The lens in the centre of this gallery was ground in 1884 by the Varnholt workshop in Lille and stood in the Skerrow Point tower for 91 years. It is a first-order Fresnel lens, 2.6 metres tall, built from 1,008 separate prisms of hand-polished crown glass. The lamp inside it burned paraffin vapour until 1936, when the tower was converted to electricity.

**Placard 1.2 — How the beam was made**

A single flame the size of a fist could be seen 31 kilometres out to sea. The lens does not magnify the flame; it bends the light from every direction into one flat beam. The whole assembly weighs 4.1 tonnes and floated on a bath of liquid mercury so that one keeper could turn it with a fingertip.

**Placard 1.3 — Please do not touch the mercury trough**

The original mercury was removed in 1998 and replaced with a sealed oil bath. The trough you see is safe to look at, but the glass prisms are fragile and every fingerprint etches the surface over time. Visitors may photograph the lens without flash.
