# Troubleshooting Off-Flavours

## Buttery or butterscotch taste
A buttery taste is diacetyl. It appears when the beer is cooled or racked before the yeast has cleaned up after itself. Fix it by warming the fermenter to 18 C for two to three days and keeping the beer on the yeast.

## Green apple taste
A green apple taste is acetaldehyde, a sign of beer that was packaged too young or yeast that was underpitched. Give the beer another week on the yeast; it normally clears with time.

## Cardboard and sherry notes
Wet cardboard or sherry flavours mean oxidation. Avoid splashing when racking, purge bottles and kegs with carbon dioxide, and drink hoppy beers fresh within six weeks.

## Medicinal or band-aid phenols
A plastic or band-aid flavour is usually chlorophenol from chlorinated tap water combined with sanitiser residue. Dechlorinate water with half a crushed Campden tablet per 20 litres and rinse equipment thoroughly.
